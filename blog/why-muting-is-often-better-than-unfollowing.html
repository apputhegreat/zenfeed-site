Why muting is often better than unfollowing, and when it isn't

Unfollowing feels final, like you're ending something. Muting feels like you're hiding from someone. Neither reputation is quite right — here's when each one is actually the tool you need.

Zenfeed — built by one person · Live on the Chrome Web Store

Most people default to one of these two moves and stick with it for every situation. Some people never unfollow anyone, because it feels rude, so they just scroll past whatever shows up. Other people unfollow the moment someone's posts start to annoy them, because muting feels like a half-measure — like you're avoiding a conversation instead of just having it. Both habits solve the wrong problem some of the time, because muting and unfollowing aren't the same tool. They're built for different situations, and picking the wrong one is why your feed still feels cluttered even after you've "dealt with" the account that was bothering you.

What's the real difference between muting and unfollowing?

Muting is quiet and reversible: their posts stop showing up in your feed, but the relationship itself doesn't change, and they're never told. Unfollowing is more visible and harder to walk back — the connection or follow itself ends, not just what shows up when you scroll.

That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should, because it decides who finds out. Mute someone and they keep seeing your posts, keep following you, and have no idea their posts stopped reaching you. Unfollow someone and the relationship visibly changes on both sides. For a coworker, a client, or a family member, that difference is often the whole decision.

Muting fits
  • Someone you're connected to in real life, but whose posting style you don't want in your feed
  • A phase — someone posting a lot about one topic for a few weeks
  • Any account where unfollowing would be noticed, and you'd rather it wasn't
Unfollowing fits
  • An account you followed for a reason that no longer applies
  • Someone you don't actually know and have no relationship to preserve
  • A pattern that's permanent, not a phase, and you're sure you won't miss it

Why does the relationship even matter if the outcome — I stop seeing their posts — is the same?

Because the outcome usually isn't the same. Unfollowing a real connection can end up being read as a small social signal, whether or not you meant it that way, and that's a cost muting doesn't carry. If the account is a stranger or a page you followed on a whim, that cost doesn't exist, and unfollowing is simply the cleaner, more permanent fix.

I think about this a lot for LinkedIn specifically, since it's built on the assumption that your feed is mostly people you know professionally, at least loosely. Muting someone there — where the platform calls it "unfollow" but the connection stays intact — lets you quiet a colleague's posts without anything showing up that could read as a snub. On X, the same logic applies in reverse: you likely don't know most of the accounts you follow, so there's rarely a relationship worth protecting, and unfollowing is the simpler default.

The choice isn't "which one is nicer." It's "does a relationship exist here that's worth keeping quiet about," and the two tools answer that question in opposite ways.

So why doesn't Zenfeed just add a mute button?

Because muting and unfollowing both act on a person, and most of what clutters a feed isn't coming from a person you'd choose to mute. Promoted posts, "people you may know" suggestions, AI-generated posts from people you do follow, and company-page updates mixed into your connections — none of that has a single person attached that muting would actually fix.

Zenfeed's custom keyword blocking works the other way around: it filters by what a post is about, not who posted it, so a phrase you're tired of seeing gets hidden no matter which of your connections happens to post it that week. That's genuinely a different job from muting or unfollowing, not a replacement for either one — unfollowing only fixes one of the four things crowding a LinkedIn feed, and a keyword filter was never going to fix the other three either, since none of them come down to a single account worth muting.

Custom keywords

Hide posts matching your own word list, case-insensitively, regardless of who posted them.

Hide promoted

Collapses sponsored posts on LinkedIn and "Ad" / "Promoted" tweets on X — no account to mute, since it's an ad slot, not a person.

Hide suggested

"People you may know," "Who to follow" — cards from LinkedIn and X's own recommendation engine, not from anyone you follow.

Rest from AI

A timer that hides AI-flagged posts for a set number of minutes, even from people you'd never mute for anything else.

Use both. Mute or unfollow the specific people whose posting style you don't want, and let a keyword or category filter handle the noise that isn't tied to a person at all. They're not competing solutions to the same problem — they're solving two different ones that happen to show up in the same scroll.

Where this lives today Zenfeed is at version 1.2.1 as of this post, live on the Chrome Web Store for LinkedIn, X, and YouTube. It runs 100% locally — no backend, no tracking, no account — so none of this involves sending your follow list or your feed anywhere.

None of this is really about etiquette, even though it can feel that way in the moment. It's about matching the tool to the actual problem: a relationship you want to keep but a feed you don't want to see, versus a category of content that has nothing to do with any one person. Muting and unfollowing were built for the first kind of problem. They were never going to solve the second, no matter how carefully you used them.

If you use LinkedIn, X, or YouTube and have a mute-versus-unfollow rule of your own, I'd like to hear it — the ones I use came from exactly that kind of trial and error.
