Four different bets on fixing a feed, and the one none of them make

SocialFocus, LinkOff, Clarity, and Control Panel for Twitter each go deep on a different axis — breadth, granularity, crowdsourcing, or interface control. None of them bet on the same combination Zenfeed does.

Zenfeed — built by one person · Live on the Chrome Web Store

Zenfeed isn't the only Chrome extension trying to fix a cluttered feed, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. SocialFocus, LinkOff, Clarity, and Control Panel for Twitter all exist for roughly the reason Zenfeed does — someone got tired of what their feed had become and built something about it. I went and looked at what each one actually ships, not just what it claims to do, because the four of them turn out to bet on four genuinely different things. Laid side by side, the gap between them is more interesting than any single feature comparison.

What does SocialFocus bet on?

SocialFocus bets on breadth. One extension covers YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, X, and Reddit, with its own set of granular toggles per platform for recommended videos, Shorts, comments, sidebars, and stories. It has no content-level AI detection and no LinkedIn coverage at all, so the trade is reach across platforms over depth on any single one of them.

That's a real and useful bet for someone who's logged into all five of those sites and wants one settings panel instead of five. It's also a bet against going deep anywhere — SocialFocus ships daily usage limits per site, not a filter that reasons about what's actually in a post.

What does LinkOff bet on?

LinkOff bets the opposite way: depth over breadth. It works on LinkedIn only, but goes further there than Zenfeed does — filtering by content type, blocking specific companies or people by name, and removing old posts. It has no AI detection and no timer, so once hidden, a post stays hidden, with no built-in way to bring it back.

If LinkedIn is the only feed you're trying to fix, that specificity is a genuine advantage over anything Zenfeed offers there. It's just a bet made at the cost of ever touching X or YouTube.

What does Clarity bet on?

Clarity bets on crowdsourcing. It auto-detects suspected AI-generated videos on YouTube, and lets people label channels into a shared blocklist meant to get smarter as more people use it. That bet requires sending data to a server to build the shared list, which is the one thing Zenfeed's local-only design rules out.

I wrote about this trade-off in more detail in why Clarity's community blocklist isn't coming to Zenfeed — it's a genuinely good idea, and Zenfeed's answer isn't that crowdsourcing is wrong, just that it needs a backend Zenfeed has never had.

Does Control Panel for Twitter detect AI content at all?

No — its Grok toggle only hides the AI chatbot button in X's interface, not posts written by AI. Its actual bet is interface control: defaulting to the chronological timeline instead of "For You," hiding that tab entirely, hiding engagement counts, and offering deep customization of layout, font, and density.

That's a different problem than filtering what shows up in the feed, and it's one Control Panel for Twitter solves better than Zenfeed does for X specifically. Its install base is also much larger than any other tool in this comparison, which is its own kind of evidence that plenty of people just want their timeline put back the way it used to work.

Four bets, side by side

SocialFocus — breadth

Five platforms, granular per-site toggles, daily usage limits. No AI detection, no LinkedIn.

LinkOff — granularity

One platform, filtered deeply by content type and account. No AI detection, no timer.

Clarity — crowdsourcing

A community blocklist that improves over time. Needs a server to work at all.

Control Panel — interface

Timeline and UI control on X. Doesn't look at what a post actually says.

What combination does none of them make?

None of the four combine working across more than one platform, a timer that hands everything back automatically, and running entirely in the browser with nothing sent to a server. Each bets hard on one axis — SocialFocus's breadth, LinkOff's granularity, Clarity's crowdsourcing, Control Panel's interface depth — and each trade-off costs something real.

Zenfeed's bet is that reversible, local, and more-than-one-platform matter more together than winning any single axis outright. It works on LinkedIn, X, and YouTube, though it does less on YouTube than on the other two — Rest from AI, custom keywords, and an opt-in Shorts toggle there, versus the fuller promoted and suggested filtering on LinkedIn and X. Posts aren't deleted, they're collapsed with a reason attached, and anything hidden by a timer comes back on its own once that timer ends.

Where Zenfeed is actually behind LinkOff's LinkedIn-specific filters go further than Zenfeed's. Control Panel for Twitter's timeline and UI controls go further on X. Clarity's community blocklist improves with scale in a way a local keyword list never will. None of that changes with this post — it's just the honest shape of what each tool is actually betting on.

None of this is a claim that detecting AI content is a quality judgment any of these tools, including Zenfeed's own Rest from AI, gets right every time. It's a volume problem — there's enough AI-written and AI-generated content now that it crowds out the posts from people you actually follow, and a keyword sweep that reads text, not pixels, will always miss some and occasionally flag one that isn't. That's true of Zenfeed's own detection as much as anyone else's.

Four extensions, four different bets, and none of them wrong for the person they're built for. Zenfeed, now on version 1.3.0, is betting that you'd rather have fewer axes done well together than one axis done better alone. If that trade doesn't fit what you need, one of the other three probably will — and that's a fine outcome too.

If you use one of these four and think I got something about it wrong, tell me. I'd rather correct it than leave a stale comparison up.
