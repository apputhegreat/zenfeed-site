Zenfeed vs CleanedIn

These two solve the same annoyance in genuinely different ways. CleanedIn sends each post to a language model in the cloud and asks it what kind of post this is. Zenfeed matches keywords and page structure in your browser and never sends anything anywhere. That one difference explains almost everything else about them, including the price.

Why someone might choose Zenfeed

Nothing you read gets sent anywhere. That matters more than it sounds, because a LinkedIn feed contains posts written by your connections, and a cloud classifier has to transmit that text to judge it. Zenfeed does the matching in the browser, so the question never arises. It is also free with no cap, needs no account, and covers X and YouTube as well as LinkedIn.

The trade is bluntness. Keyword matching cannot tell a thoughtful post from a shallow one, so Zenfeed filters by category rather than by quality.

Why someone might still want CleanedIn

A language model reading each post can make judgements a keyword list simply cannot. Recognising engagement bait, a humble brag, or empty inspiration is a question of tone, not vocabulary, and that is exactly what CleanedIn's ten categories are built around. Its Spotlight feature also highlights posts worth reading rather than only hiding the rest, which is something Zenfeed does not attempt at all.

If you only use LinkedIn, you want filtering by quality rather than by category, and you are comfortable with post text being classified in the cloud, it is doing something Zenfeed deliberately does not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does CleanedIn send your LinkedIn feed to a server?

Yes, by design. Its privacy policy states that post text is sent over HTTPS to its own edge function, which forwards it to Anthropic's Claude API for classification, then discards it. Zenfeed never sends post text anywhere, because it matches keywords in your browser instead of asking a model.

Is there a free alternative to CleanedIn with no daily limit?

Zenfeed is free with no paid tier, no account, and no cap on how many posts it filters. CleanedIn's free plan allows 20 classifications a day before you need a paid plan, which is the normal trade when a cloud model is doing the work and each post costs the provider money.
