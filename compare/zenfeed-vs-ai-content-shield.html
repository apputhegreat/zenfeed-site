Zenfeed vs AI Content Shield

AI Content Shield blocks AI-generated content across a very wide range of sites — well beyond what Zenfeed covers. Zenfeed stays focused on LinkedIn, X, and YouTube, and adds promoted/suggested filtering plus a timed Rest from AI window. Here's how they actually compare.

Why someone might choose Zenfeed

Zenfeed is fully free with no subscription tier, and pairs AI detection with promoted/suggested post filtering and custom keyword blocking, which aren't part of AI Content Shield's stated feature set. The Rest from AI timer is also a different mechanic entirely — a scheduled break that ends and restores itself, rather than an always-on filter.

Why someone might still want AI Content Shield

AI Content Shield's platform coverage is far broader — Reddit, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, Pinterest, Gmail, Amazon, eBay, general web, and Google's AI Mode/AI Overviews are all places Zenfeed doesn't reach at all. It also has a larger install base and rating (4.4★, 222 ratings, 10,000+ users), which points to a longer track record than Zenfeed's.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Zenfeed or AI Content Shield cover more platforms?

AI Content Shield covers far more ground — it works across Google's AI Mode and AI Overviews, YouTube, TikTok, X, Instagram, Facebook, Threads, Reddit, Pinterest, LinkedIn, Gmail, Amazon, eBay, and general web pages. Zenfeed only works on LinkedIn, X, and YouTube. If broad coverage across many sites matters most to you, AI Content Shield reaches further. If you mainly live in those three feeds and want more control features there, Zenfeed goes deeper on them instead.

Which extension is better for blocking AI content on YouTube?

Both work on YouTube, but differently. AI Content Shield blocks AI-generated content there continuously, with no time limit. Zenfeed's Rest from AI is a timed break — you set it for 15, 30, or 60 minutes, or a custom length, and everything comes back automatically when the timer ends. If you want AI content hidden permanently, AI Content Shield's always-on approach fits that better. If you just want a temporary break, Zenfeed's timer is the more direct fit.
